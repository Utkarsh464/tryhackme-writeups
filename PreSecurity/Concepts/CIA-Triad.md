# CIA Triad

## Definition
The CIA Triad is a foundational cybersecurity model comprising three principles: **Confidentiality** (ensuring data is accessible only to authorized parties), **Integrity** (ensuring data is accurate and unaltered), and **Availability** (ensuring systems and data are accessible when needed). These three pillars guide security policies, controls, and risk management decisions.

## Why It Matters
Every security control maps back to one or more CIA principles. Encryption supports confidentiality, hashing supports integrity, and redundancy supports availability. Understanding CIA helps you analyze threats, design defenses, and communicate security requirements clearly.

## Key Points
- Confidentiality: encryption, access control, least privilege
- Integrity: checksums, digital signatures, version control
- Availability: redundancy, failover, DDoS mitigation
- Balancing the triad often requires trade-offs

## Common Interview Questions
1. What is the CIA Triad?
**Answer:** A model of Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability guiding information security.
2. Give an example where Availability conflicts with Confidentiality.
**Answer:** Allowing more access for uptime (availability) may reduce confidentiality protections.
