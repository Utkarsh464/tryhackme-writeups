# Training Impact on Teams — Tasks

## Task 1: Why Training Matters
Explore the human element in cybersecurity. Review statistics on data breaches caused by human error, phishing susceptibility rates, and the cost of insider threats. Understand that technology alone cannot prevent attacks — people must be part of the defense.

## Task 2: Security Awareness Training
Learn about security awareness programs for non-technical employees. Topics include password hygiene, phishing recognition, tailgating prevention, clean desk policy, and reporting suspicious activity. Discuss how to make awareness training engaging and how often it should be delivered.

## Task 3: Phishing Simulations
Understand how simulated phishing campaigns work. Learn to design campaigns with different payload types (credential harvesting, malware attachment, vishing). Explore metrics: click rate, report rate, and repeat clickers. Understand how simulation results inform targeted training.

## Task 4: Tabletop Exercises
Learn how tabletop exercises simulate incident scenarios in a discussion-based format. Design a scenario (e.g., ransomware infection, data breach, insider threat) and walk through the response steps. Understand the role of facilitators, observers, and participants from different departments.

## Task 5: Red Team vs. Blue Team Exercises
Explore full-scale adversarial simulations. The red team acts as attackers trying to breach defenses, while the blue team detects and responds. Understand how these exercises test detection capabilities, response procedures, and communication under pressure. Learn about purple teaming as a collaborative approach.

## Task 6: The ADDIE Model for Training Design
Apply the ADDIE instructional design framework: Analysis (identify training needs and gaps), Design (define learning objectives and content), Development (create materials and exercises), Implementation (deliver the training), and Evaluation (assess effectiveness). Build a sample training plan using this model.

## Task 7: Measuring Training Effectiveness
Explore metrics for evaluating training: phishing click-through rates before and after training, mean time to detect (MTTD), mean time to respond (MTTR), number of security incidents reported by employees, and scores on knowledge assessments. Understand how to calculate return on security training investment.

## Task 8: Skills Matrices and Team Development
Learn how to build a skills matrix that maps team members against required competencies (SIEM operations, malware analysis, cloud security, etc.). Use the matrix to identify skill gaps, plan training, and track professional development over time.

## Task 9: Building a Security Culture
Understand the difference between compliance-driven training (checking a box) and building a security culture (security as a shared value). Explore how leadership buy-in, rewards for secure behavior, open communication, and continuous learning contribute to a strong security culture.

## Task 10: Creating a Training Program Proposal
Synthesize everything learned. Develop a proposal for a training program at a fictional organization. Define the audience, identify the key risks to address, select training methods (awareness, simulations, exercises), propose metrics for success, and estimate the budget and resources required.
