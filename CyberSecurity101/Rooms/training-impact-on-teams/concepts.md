# Training Impact on Teams — Concepts

## Security Awareness Training
A program designed to educate all employees about security risks and their role in protecting organizational assets. Topics include recognizing phishing emails, creating strong passwords, securing devices, safe internet browsing, and reporting incidents. Effective awareness training is continuous, engaging, and tailored to different roles within the organization.

## Phishing Simulations
Controlled exercises where organizations send fake phishing emails to their employees to test their ability to recognize and report malicious messages. Simulations measure click rates, credential submission rates, and report rates. Results are used to identify high-risk individuals or departments and to target additional training where needed.

## Tabletop Exercises
Discussion-based sessions where participants walk through their response to a simulated security incident in a low-pressure environment. Unlike live exercises, tabletops do not involve actual technology — they focus on decision-making, communication, coordination, and identification of gaps in plans and procedures.

## Red vs. Blue Team Exercises
Adversarial simulations where the red team (attackers) attempts to breach defenses while the blue team (defenders) detects and responds. Red teams use the same tools and techniques as real adversaries. Blue teams use monitoring tools, SIEM platforms, and response procedures. Purple teaming is a collaborative model where red and blue teams work together to improve detection and response.

## ADDIE Model
A systematic instructional design framework used to develop effective training programs. The five phases are: Analysis (identify the problem, learners, and context), Design (define learning objectives, content structure, and assessment methods), Development (create training materials and exercises), Implementation (deliver the training to learners), and Evaluation (measure effectiveness and identify improvements).

## Training ROI
Return on investment for security training is calculated by comparing the cost of training (development, delivery, employee time) against the reduction in security incidents and their associated costs. While difficult to measure precisely, leading indicators include reduced phishing click rates, faster incident reporting, and improved detection times.

## Security Culture
The set of values, beliefs, and behaviors that shape how an organization approaches security. A strong security culture means employees proactively consider security in their daily work, feel comfortable reporting mistakes, and understand that security is everyone's responsibility — not just the IT department's.
