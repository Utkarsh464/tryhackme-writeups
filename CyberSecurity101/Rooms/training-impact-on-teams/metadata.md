# Room: Training Impact on Teams

## URL
https://tryhackme.com/room/trainingimpactonteams

## Description
Cybersecurity is not just about technology — it is about people. Even the most sophisticated security controls can be undermined by untrained or unaware employees. This room examines how cybersecurity training programs improve team performance, reduce human error, and strengthen an organization's overall security posture. Learners will explore the different types of security training: security awareness training for all employees, technical skills development for IT and security teams, and specialized training for incident response and red team members. The room covers how phishing simulations help reduce susceptibility to social engineering attacks, how tabletop exercises test incident response plans without deploying technology, and how red team vs. blue team exercises provide realistic attack and defense scenarios. Learners will understand how to build a security training program using the ADDIE model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, Evaluation) and how to measure training effectiveness through metrics such as phishing click rates, detection times, and mean time to respond. The room also explores the concept of security culture — how organizations move from compliance-driven training to building a culture where security is everyone's responsibility. Additional topics include skills matrices for tracking team competencies, the role of gamification and capture-the-flag events in building skills, and strategies for training budget justification and ROI calculation.

## Objectives
- Differentiate between security awareness training and technical skills development
- Describe how phishing simulations reduce social engineering risk
- Explain the purpose of tabletop exercises in incident readiness
- Understand red team vs. blue team exercise design
- Apply the ADDIE model to design a basic training program
- Identify metrics for measuring training effectiveness and ROI
- Describe how security culture differs from security compliance
