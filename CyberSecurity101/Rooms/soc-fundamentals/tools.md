# SOC Fundamentals - Tools

## SIEM (Security Information and Event Management)
SIEM platforms are the central nervous system of a SOC. They collect log data from across the organization, normalize it into a consistent format, and provide search, visualization, and alerting capabilities. Splunk Enterprise Security is a leading commercial SIEM known for its powerful search processing language (SPL), customizable dashboards, and extensive app ecosystem. The ELK Stack (Elasticsearch, Logstash, Kibana) is the most popular open-source SIEM, offering scalable log storage, flexible log parsing, and rich visualization. IBM QRadar provides strong correlation capabilities and built-in threat intelligence. Microsoft Sentinel is a cloud-native SIEM that integrates deeply with the Microsoft security ecosystem. SIEM analysts spend their time writing queries, investigating alerts, creating dashboards, and tuning correlation rules to reduce false positives.

## EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response)
EDR platforms provide real-time visibility into endpoint activity, including process execution, file system changes, network connections, registry modifications, and memory operations. CrowdStrike Falcon uses a lightweight agent and cloud-based analytics for AI-powered threat detection. SentinelOne provides autonomous response capabilities, automatically containing threats without human intervention. Microsoft Defender for Endpoint integrates with the Windows ecosystem and provides deep visibility into Windows endpoints. Carbon Black (VMware) offers extensive query capabilities for threat hunting. EDR tools are essential for detecting fileless malware, living-off-the-land attacks, and other advanced threats that traditional antivirus cannot detect.

## SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation, and Response)
SOAR platforms help SOC teams automate repetitive tasks and orchestrate complex response workflows. Splunk SOAR (formerly Phantom) provides a visual playbook editor, extensive integration with third-party tools, and case management capabilities. Palo Alto Networks XSOAR (formerly Demisto) offers a large community-contributed playbook library and strong threat intelligence management. IBM Resilient focuses on incident management and collaboration. SOAR platforms reduce mean time to respond (MTTR) by automating evidence collection, indicator enrichment, containment actions, and notification workflows.

## Ticketing and Case Management
SOC operations depend on robust ticketing systems for tracking alerts and incidents from creation through resolution. ServiceNow is a widely used enterprise IT service management platform with security incident response modules. Jira Service Management provides flexible workflow customization for security teams. RTIR (Request Tracker for Incident Response) is an open-source alternative specifically designed for incident response. These systems track the status of each incident, document actions taken, provide audit trails, and generate reports on SOC performance metrics.

## Threat Intelligence Platforms
Threat Intelligence Platforms (TIPs) aggregate, correlate, and analyze threat data from multiple sources. MISP (Malware Information Sharing Platform) is an open-source threat intelligence platform used by many SOCs and ISACs for sharing indicators and threat intelligence. ThreatConnect provides commercial threat intelligence management with orchestration capabilities. Recorded Future offers AI-powered threat intelligence with real-time risk scoring. TIPs enable SOCs to operationalize threat intelligence by feeding indicators directly into SIEM and firewall systems for automated detection and blocking.
