# Networking Essentials — Concepts

## Router
A Layer 3 device that forwards packets between different networks. Routers maintain routing tables and make forwarding decisions based on IP addresses.

## Switch
A Layer 2 device that forwards frames based on MAC addresses. Switches create dedicated communication paths between devices, improving efficiency over hubs.

## Hub
A simple Layer 1 device that repeats incoming signals to all ports. All devices share the same collision domain, making hubs inefficient for modern networks.

## LAN vs. WAN
A Local Area Network (LAN) covers a small geographic area. A Wide Area Network (WAN) spans cities or countries and connects multiple LANs.

## Network Topology
The physical or logical arrangement of devices. Common topologies include star (central switch), bus (single cable), ring (circular path), and mesh (fully interconnected).

## VLAN (Virtual LAN)
A logical subdivision of a switch that isolates traffic at Layer 2. VLANs reduce broadcast domains and improve security without requiring separate physical hardware.

## Collision Domain vs. Broadcast Domain
A collision domain is a network segment where packet collisions can occur. A broadcast domain is a segment where a broadcast frame is received by all devices. Switches reduce collision domains; VLANs reduce broadcast domains.
